The logo

Gudiparan, the Bracket Kite

Two code brackets, < and >, make an Afghan kite. Learners build it; the community holds the string.

A kite made from parts

A gudiparan, the Afghan fighter kite, is made from simple parts with patience. It flies on weekends and rises because someone holds the string. CodeWeekend works the same way: learners build the thing and fly it, and mentors, alumni and donors hold the string.

Every angle comes from girih tile geometry. The kite is always a made object. We never draw a flyer or hands.

Text pairs that pass

Every text colour meets WCAG AA. These are the pairs we use most.

  • Aa Ink on Paper 15.1 : 1
  • Aa Lapis on Paper 7.5 : 1
  • Aa Paper on Night 16.7 : 1
  • Aa Saffron on Night 8.9 : 1
  • Aa Night on Saffron 8.9 : 1

Never

  • Saffron text on Paper (1.9 : 1). Use Saffron for shapes, not words, on light grounds.
  • The Lapis logo on Night (2.2 : 1). Use the reverse logo.
  • Green, anywhere. Success states use Lapis with a check icon.
  • Lapis and Saffron as two equal stripes, or any flag-like band.
  • Gradients, glows or glass effects. All colour is flat.

Usage

Give the kite room to fly

Use the files as supplied. If you need a version that is not here, write to us and we will make it.

Do

  • Use the full-colour logo on light grounds and the reverse logo on dark ones.
  • Keep the tail. Every version of the logo has one.
  • Keep the logo left to right, also on Dari and Pashto pages.
  • Write our name as CodeWeekend in running text.

Smallest sizes

116 px wide on screen · 29 mm in print

79 px tall on screen
Kite mark
20 px tall · 5 mm in print

Please don't

  • Stretch or squash it.

  • Rotate or tilt it.

  • Add shadows, outlines or glows.

  • Place it on a Saffron field.

  • Use the full-colour logo on Night.
