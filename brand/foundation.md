# CodeWeekend brand foundation

Version 1.0 · 2026-10-03 · Brand director merge of three strategy lenses

**How this was merged.** The *heritage & craft* lens is the backbone, because "code is a craft, passed hand to hand" links Afghan making traditions (lapis, girih tile, kilim, kites) to what CodeWeekend does every weekend. From the *developer culture* lens we kept the builder's voice: ship, show the product, use real numbers, the live cursor, and the changelog idiom used sparingly. From the *human hope* lens we kept the dignity rules, the community as the kite's string, the "Her Kite" portraits and the sign-off line. Cut: stacked metaphors, terminal cosplay in body copy, the dawn-apricot surface (too soft beside saffron), a second display face, Martian Mono and Instrument Sans (one type family voice is stronger), and every image of a person.

---

## 1. Essence

> **Code is a craft, passed hand to hand.**
> CodeWeekend is a free, live coding guild where Afghan women and girls learn to build real software, one weekend at a time, with a worldwide community holding the string.

**Brand idea: lift, made by hand.** A gudiparan (fighter kite) is made from simple parts with patience. It is flown on weekends, taught by older flyers to younger ones, and it rises because someone holds the string. CodeWeekend works the same way. Learners make the thing and fly it. The community of mentors, alumni and donors holds the string. Nobody is rescued; everybody builds.

**Proof points (always available to back a claim):** running since 2014 (Kabul meetup → registered non-profit society in British Columbia, Canada) · free, fully live, instructor-led · 12-week Web & AI Development Bootcamp for women and girls in Afghanistan · about 30 graduates showing working products at Demo Day 2026 · freeCodeCamp called it "Afghanistan's friendliest coding club".

**Line system**

| Role | Line | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Positioning | An inclusive community of developers. | Under the logo, OG cards, footer |
| Hero | Change your life, learn to code. | Home hero (keep) |
| Sign-off | Keep learning. Keep building. Together. | Footer, emails, end of decks |
| Craft line (max one per page) | One pattern at a time. | Programme pages, curriculum |
| Continuity line (donors, impact, press) | The code goes on. | Impact report, appeals |

In running text the name is always **CodeWeekend** (one word, camel case). Only the logo wordmark is lowercase.

## 2. Personality

1. **Warm host.** "Afghanistan's friendliest coding club." The tea is poured before the lesson starts. Every question is welcome, and people are known by name.
2. **Maker.** We ship. We show products, repositories and Demo Day, not promises. The work is the proof.
3. **Patient and precise.** A weaver's patience and an engineer's exactness. Line by line, real numbers, nothing sloppy, because craft is a form of respect.
4. **Steadfast.** Kabul 2014, through 2021, still teaching every weekend. Calm and durable, never dramatic.
5. **Proud, not pitied.** Learners are authors and builders. Afghan culture is shown with confidence, never as exotic or as tragedy.
6. **Hopeful with facts.** The kite's lift: optimism, always with a number attached.

## 3. Voice and tone

**Voice in one line:** a warm senior developer on a Saturday morning, part mentor and part older sister. Plain, direct, encouraging, specific.

### Writing rules
1. **Plain English first.** Aim for CEFR B1. Keep sentences under about 20 words and use active verbs. No idioms or puns, because they break in Google Translate to Dari and Pashto ("hit the ground running", "crush it").
2. **Craft verbs, not hype verbs.** Build, make, practise, ship, finish, pattern, stitch together. Never use disrupt, revolutionise, leverage, ninja, rockstar or 10x.
3. **Numbers over adjectives.** "30 graduates showed 30 working apps after 12 weeks", not "amazing results".
4. **The learner is the subject of the sentence.** "Mahdia built a clinic-booking app." Never "we saved", "giving voice to the voiceless" or "beneficiaries".
5. **Name the education ban once, plainly and factually, when it is relevant.** Then move straight to what learners are doing. No trauma detail and no "plight".
6. **Developer idiom is seasoning: at most one per section.** Eyebrows can read like code comments (`// demo day`) and the impact timeline like release notes (`v2014 → v2026`). Never write body copy as a fake terminal.
7. **At most one craft metaphor per page.** Never stack them. Never "ancient", "mystical", "land of".
8. **Use exclamation marks rarely and numbers often.** Use first names only, and only with consent. Never give a detail that could locate a learner.

### Tone by audience

| Audience | Tone | Sample |
|---|---|---|
| Learners | Second person, inviting, concrete next step | "Your first line of code is this weekend. You'll publish your first website in week 3." |
| Donors and funders | Plain, accountable, evidence first | "Fund a seat. $X keeps one learner online and learning for 12 weeks." |
| Mentors | Peer to peer, a small concrete ask | "Give one evening a week. Code review, pair programming, office hours. Watch someone ship." |
| Employers | Spec sheet | "Hire graduates who have already shipped. Stack: HTML, CSS, JavaScript, React, Git, AI tools. 120+ live hours. Capstone in production." |
| Press | Factual, quotable, numbers | "Since 2014, CodeWeekend has taught free, live coding classes to Afghan learners. In 2026, about 30 women showed working products at Demo Day." |

### Do and don't

| Don't | Do |
|---|---|
| "Empowering voiceless Afghan girls to escape darkness." | "Afghan women are building web apps. We teach them, live and free." |
| "We give these brave girls a chance." | "Graduates lead their own projects. Mahdia built a booking app for a clinic." |
| "Amazing results this year!!!" | "30 graduates. 30 working products. One stage." |
| "Leverage cutting-edge AI to revolutionise your future." | "Learn to build websites and use AI tools well. 12 weeks, live, free." |

## 4. Colour

### Principles
- **Lapis** (Badakhshan lapis lazuli) is the brand. **Saffron** (Herat) is the light: the kite panel, the cursor, the one bright thing on the page. **Pomegranate** (Kandahar) is for celebration and urgency, used sparingly. **Firuza** (Herati tile turquoise) is a quiet detail colour for tags and illustration.
- Surfaces are warm wool paper, never clinical white. Dark sections are **Night**, a blue-black, never pure black.
- Suggested share of any layout: paper surfaces about 60%, ink and night about 22%, lapis about 12%, saffron 5% or less, pomegranate 3% or less, firuza 2% or less.
- All colour is flat. No gradients, glows or glass effects.

### Cultural guardrails (non-negotiable)
- **There is no green in the palette.** Success states use Lapis plus a check icon. The black, red and green trio cannot form.
- Firuza is deliberately a *blue* turquoise (HSL hue 192°). Never place it as an equal band beside Pomegranate on Night or Ink, and never build a three-stripe band from Night, Pomegranate and Firuza.
- Never stack Lapis and Saffron as two equal horizontal fields, because that reads as a national flag. Saffron is always an accent, never a co-equal field.
- No flags, no large decorative calligraphy, no religious geometry (eight-point stars, arches or mihrabs).

## 5. Typography

### Families (all on Google Fonts)

| Role | Family | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Display | **Bricolage Grotesque** (variable opsz 12–96, wght 500–800) | A grotesque with ink traps and slightly irregular, hand-cut terminals: visibly *made*, like stamp-printed textile or chiselled wood. Its name means "building with what is at hand", which is the CodeWeekend story. Its low contrast and sturdy, open forms sit well beside Vazirmatn. It replaces the generic startup-serif look of Fraunces. |
| Body and UI | **Atkinson Hyperlegible Next** (400, 400 italic, 600, 700) | Designed by the Braille Institute so that easily confused characters look different (I/l/1, O/0, rn/m). That matters for a learner reading her second or third language on a small, low-resolution Android phone, and twice as much for a beginner coder. It is excellent at 16–17px. |
| Mono | **Atkinson Hyperlegible Mono** (400, 500, 600) | Carries the same character distinction into code, where 0/O and 1/l are real bugs. Prose and code read as one voice. |
| RTL (Dari, Pashto) | **Vazirmatn** (400, 500, 700, 800) | Kept. It replaces all three Latin families when `dir="rtl"`. |

**RTL rules.** Under `[dir=rtl]`, map display, body and mono to Vazirmatn, with headings at 800 and body at 400. Increase body size about 6% and set line-height to 1.8. Set **`letter-spacing: 0` and `text-transform: none` everywhere, because letter-spacing breaks Arabic-script joining.** The logo and code blocks stay `dir="ltr"`.

**Performance budget.** Load the Latin subset only, with `display=swap`. Bricolage ships as one variable file for 500–800. The total is about the same as the old Fraunces, Inter and JetBrains set.

## 6. Imagery: "Tile, Thread & Kite"

**In one line:** diagrams of making, not pictures of people. Flat vector, built on a grid, small enough to inline as SVG.

### Rules
1. Every illustration sits on a hidden construction grid: the 10-fold girih set (rhombus, bowtie, pentagon and decagon angles in multiples of 36°) or a square kilim knot grid. Shapes snap to it. Corners are crisp tile vertices or stepped weaving edges, never blobby "corporate Memphis" curves.
2. Use flat fills only: no gradients, glows, drop shadows, noise or glassmorphism. A sky is 3–5 stacked flat bands, Night → Lapis-900 → Lapis-800, or Paper → Sand on light.
3. Use 2–4 palette colours per piece. Night or Lapis give depth, Saffron is the single light source, and Pomegranate or Firuza is one detail at most. Paper is the negative space.
4. Strokes are 2px at 1×, with 8px corner radius on windows and cards. Draw every piece for both Paper and Night.
5. Hero SVGs stay under about 25KB. Spot illustrations stay under about 6KB.

### Hero art: "Kites over the Hindu Kush" (replaces `static/images/hero.svg`; keep the 800×1000 portrait viewBox)
A Night sky in flat bands. Three faceted ridgelines of the Hindu Kush along the bottom in Night-2 → Lapis-800 → Lapis-600, drawn with girih-angled peaks. The front ridge steps down like indented code. A large Saffron sun disc sits low behind the middle ridge. High in the sky flies the logo kite, with its stitched-dash tail. Six to ten smaller kites at different heights (Saffron, Pomegranate-300, Lapis-200, Firuza-300, Paper) suggest a cohort. Every kite string is a hairline that runs off the bottom edge, so we never see who holds them: many hands, nobody identifiable. Along the very bottom runs a thin kilim border band of stepped lozenges in Saffron, Firuza-300 and Paper, built from tiny code glyphs (`< > { } / ;`). The mobile crop keeps the sun, the logo kite and two kites. A light variant (Lapis mountains on Paper and Sand bands) is available for light sections.

### Story "portraits" without faces: "Her Kite" name tiles (also replaces `students-photo/placeholder.svg`)
Each graduate can have a square or 4:5 tile: a solid field (rotating Lapis-600, Night, Sand or Paper-2) with one large kite. The kite's four panels are patterned by deterministic rules from a hash of a consented first name or cohort ID: stripes, chevrons, pomegranate-seed dots, a kilim step, or solid. Colours come from five approved pairs: Lapis + Saffron, Night + Firuza-300, Sand + Pomegranate-600, Paper-2 + Lapis-600, Night + Saffron. Her initial is set large on the kite in Bricolage 800 (Vazirmatn 800 for Dari names). A small object icon in one corner names what she built (film strip for a movie platform, shopping bag for e-commerce, book for an education app, calendar for booking). Build it as a Hugo partial that outputs inline SVG: no image files and no privacy risk. On Demo Day, 30 graduates become 30 kites, and the "kite wall" is the cohort photo.

### Photography
- **Default to Her Kite tiles and real product screenshots.** Screenshots are the proof: show them in a simple flat browser or phone frame drawn in Night or Paper, with a 1px `--line-strong` border.
- Real photos of learners appear **only with explicit, informed, revocable consent**, decided person by person by the team, and never as decoration in brand illustration. Where consented graduate photos already appear (for example on Demo Day), frame them consistently: square, 14px radius, a 1px `--line` border, no filters or blur. Blur stigmatises, and it does not protect anyone.
- Mentors, staff and partners outside Afghanistan who consent may use real photos. Duotone (Night + Paper) is optional for consistency.
- Spot illustrations show objects, never people: a phone with a code editor, a tea glass beside a laptop, a notebook with a wireframe, a kite, a pomegranate cut open to show a grid of seeds.

## 7. Logo directions

All five directions meet the hard constraints: 16px favicon, light and dark, mono, a horizontal lockup for the 64px nav, a square avatar, and always LTR (`dir="ltr"`, never mirrored). They are deliberately different constructions: **symbol** (1), **inline brace lockup** (2), **woven pixel pattern** (3), **pure type** (4) and **community dots** (5). Only direction 2 uses curly braces. Directions 1, 2 and 3 draw on Afghan craft geometry, and direction 4 is typographic-led.

**Recommended shortlist:** 1 Bracket Kite, 3 Kilim Commit Grid, 4 Live Cursor (three genuinely different constructions). **Recommended choice:** 1 Bracket Kite, **chosen and final** (see [`exploration/decision.md`](exploration/decision.md) and section 7.1 below). It is the most ownable and story-rich: code (`< >`), craft (kilim lozenge, girih angles) and lift (a weekend kite flown with a community holding the string), with no person depicted. At 16px it reduces to a hand-pixelled gold diamond inside a stepped Lapis-200 ring.

### 1. Gudiparan: the Bracket Kite (chosen, final)

**Status:** final. This section summarises the production kit. The public files are in `static/brand/` (favicon and app icons in `static/`), and [`logo/SPEC.md`](logo/SPEC.md) holds the full numbers, nav CSS, file index and the acceptance-test results. Where this summary and the SPEC differ, the SPEC wins.

**One-line story:** "Two code brackets, `<` and `>`, make an Afghan kite. Learners build it; the community holds the string." We present the kite as a made object (craft), not as a re-enactment of the rooftop game. Ownership is shown through the Her Kite tiles. Never draw a flyer or hands. Never write "girls fly kites now". Never reference *The Kite Runner*.

- **Units:** 1u = 1/24 of the kite's width, and y runs down. *Body height* is the outer kite polygon, 25.24u.
- **Symbol:** two heavy chevrons, `<` and `>`, with open sides facing so they outline an Afghan fighter kite. Every angle comes from the girih set: 108° top, 90° sides, 72° bottom.
  - The outer polygon is top (12, 0), right (24, 8.72), bottom (12, 25.24), left (0, 8.72).
  - The stroke is 3.6u, with mitred side vertices and flat cuts at the spine.
  - A 2u gap at the top and bottom vertices is both the bracket break and the bamboo spine. The chevron tips sit at y = 23.86u.
- **Panel:** a Saffron kite with the same angles, scaled to 34%: (12, 7.54), (16.08, 10.5), (12, 16.12), (7.92, 10.5). Its side vertices sit on the **panel axis, y = 10.5u**.
  - **Spine split:** at body heights of 48px and up, in every colourway including mono, a 1.0u vertical knockout on x = 12u splits the panel in two. It continues the spine as a bamboo spar, which removes the gem or crypto "diamond in a diamond" reading. Below 48px the panel stays solid.
- **Tail (in every lockup):** stitches are straight, flat-cut dashes in the body colour, and the tassel is a 72°/108° rhombus. The tail never hangs straight down: it always curls left at girih angles. Every gap is a true clearance.
  - **Lockup tail** (the default for lockups, `mark.svg` and bodies of 24–95px): the origin is on x = 12u, 0.8u clear of the tips. Stitches are 2.2 × 2.0u, at 54° and then 72°, with 1.0u gaps. The tassel is 3.0 × 2.2u along 72°. It adds about 5.6u below the tips.
  - **Full tail** (avatars, hero, print, stickers, OG, bodies of 96px and up): an 18°/36°/54° curl, with 2.2 × 2.4u stitches, 1.4u gaps and the tassel along 72°.
  - **No tail:** only in the favicon tile and in repeat patterns (kite lattice, Her Kite tiles).
  - **Tassel colour:** Pomegranate-600 on light and Pomegranate-300 on dark. In mono, everything takes the body colour.
- **Two size masters, no media queries:**
  - **Large** (body 48px and up): spine split, plus the lockup or full tail.
  - **Small** (body 20–47px, for example the nav): solid panel. The lockup tail has 2.6 × 2.2u stitches and a 3.6u tassel, so every element is at least 3px at a 36px body.
- **Wordmark:** `codeweekend`, lowercase, one colour (Ink on light, Paper on dark).
  - Bricolage Grotesque 800, re-instanced from the variable font at an integer opsz of 48.
  - Tracking -15/1000 em, with GPOS kerning.
  - The e apertures are opened. **The k is stock**: the slit is removed.
  - "ee", "we" and "nd" stay open at 14, 16 and 20px.
- **Lockups:**
  - **Horizontal:** the wordmark is set at 18u, with its x-height centred on the panel axis (y = 10.5u). The gap is 1.3 × stroke, and the lockup tail hangs below the kite.
  - **In the 64px nav:** use the Small master with a 36px body and the panel axis on the bar centre (`logo-small.svg` or `logo-inline.svg` at 59.06 × 208.87px). Its box is symmetric about the panel axis, so plain vertical centring works with no offsets. The tail ends at about 60px, inside the bar.
  - **Stacked:** the kite is centred over the wordmark, and the wordmark is 3.4 × the kite's width. The gap from the tips to the ascender line is 2 × stroke.
  - Every lockup comes in light, reverse and mono (ink and white). **There is no tail-less lockup.**
- **Clear space:** 2 × stroke (7.2u), measured from the visible body (and the wordmark). The tail may sit inside it.
- **Minimum sizes:** a lockup body of 20px on screen (5mm in print). For a mark below 20px, use the favicon.
- **Colour:** unchanged from section 4.
  - Paper or white ground: Lapis-600 body, Saffron-400 panel, Pomegranate-600 tassel, Ink wordmark.
  - Night, Lapis-900 or Lapis-600 ground: Paper body and wordmark, Saffron-400 panel, Pomegranate-300 tassel.
  - Never put a Lapis body on Night. Never place the logo on a saffron field. No green.
- **Favicon set:** no media queries and no tail.
  - `favicon.svg` is a vector tile on the master angles: a Night tile with a 3px radius (at 16px), a Lapis-200 kite ring, 2-unit spine gaps and a solid Saffron core. It has no rendering hints, so it stays symmetric at every DPR.
  - `favicon.ico` holds three hand-pixelled frames on the master angles. The 16 frame has a 2-4-6-4-2 Saffron diamond, no spine gap, and never a stem under the core (it turns the core into a keyhole). The 32 and 48 frames have 2px spine gaps and a solid core.
  - Also shipped: `apple-touch-icon.png`, `icon-192.png`, `icon-512.png` and `icon-maskable-512.png` (kite inside the maskable safe zone).
- **Avatar:** the default is the flying variant: 8° clockwise, with the rotation baked into the paths. It has the full tail, a Night ground, Paper chevrons, the solid saffron panel (at avatar sizes the split reads as a brown streak) and a Pomegranate-300 tassel. The body is 57% of the height, and everything sits inside the inscribed circle with an 8.9% margin, so it survives a 40px circle crop. The upright avatar is the alternate.
- **Alumni continuity:**
  - Launch line: "code{ }weekend is now codeweekend: same community, since 2014."
  - For six months the footer carries "since 2014, formerly code{ }weekend" in Atkinson Hyperlegible Mono.
  - Sub-brands use the lockup plus a Mono descriptor (`DEMO DAY 2026`), never `demo{◆}day`.

### 2. Girih Braces: code{◆}weekend
- **Symbol:** the community's existing `{ }` rebuilt as Herati girih strapwork. Each brace is one continuous straight-line band (width 1/8 of mark height, square caps, mitred bends) whose bends use only girih angles (36°, 72°, 108°, 144°): a short flat arm, a vertical shank, then a fold into a sharp 72° inward point at mid-height (the brace nub), mirrored below. On a 16u-tall grid each brace is 5u wide with a 2u band and a 6u inner gap. Between them sits one Saffron 72°/108° girih rhombus (4 × 5.5u, long axis vertical), the "content" of the code block. On applications of 96px and larger only, a 0.5u Firuza-600 keyline runs inside each band to show tile strapwork's over-and-under.
- **Lockup (inline):** `code` + symbol + `weekend`, lowercase Bricolage Grotesque 700, tracking -0.02em. The symbol height is 1.3 × cap height, and the brace tips drop 10% below the baseline like real brace glyphs. Words in Ink, braces in Lapis-600, rhombus in Saffron. This keeps the "code{ }weekend" recognition alumni have had since 2014. The braces and rhombus alone serve as avatar and favicon.
- **Colour:** on dark, braces Paper and rhombus Saffron. Mono: braces and rhombus in one colour.
- **Favicon 16px:** a Night rounded square (22% radius), two 2px Paper braces each with a single 72° point, and a 4px Saffron rhombus between them.
- **Guardrails:** no interlace that resolves into a star, and no arch or mihrab silhouette.

### 3. Kilim Gul: the Commit Grid
- **Symbol:** a 7×7 grid of square cells (1u cells, 0.25u gutters, 8.5u overall) that is both a kilim gul (stepped diamond medallion) and a GitHub contribution graph. Filled cells by (row, column) from 0: the outer ring in Lapis-600 at (0,3) (1,2) (1,4) (2,1) (2,5) (3,0) (3,6) (4,1) (4,5) (5,2) (5,4) (6,3); the inner ring in Lapis-300 `#9CBBFF` at (2,3) (3,2) (3,4) (4,3); the centre (3,3) in Saffron-400 for "this weekend's commit". Empty cells are absent, not grey, so the silhouette is a crisp stepped diamond with a woven, checkered rhythm. Cells have square corners only, so it reads woven rather than as an app icon.
- **Wordmark:** `codeweekend`, lowercase Bricolage Grotesque 700, tracking -0.02em, with "code" in Ink and "weekend" in Lapis-600 (one colour in mono). Large lockups only add a one-cell stepped "running water" kilim rule under the wordmark in Sand.
- **Lockup:** grid height 1.6 × cap height, with a gap of one cell plus gutter.
- **Colour on dark:** outer ring Lapis-300, inner ring Paper, centre Saffron, on Night.
- **Favicon 16px:** the same 7×7 at 2px cells with no gutters and a 1px margin. It is pixel-perfect with no anti-aliasing.
- **Avatar:** the grid on Night. **Mono:** one colour, with the centre cell knocked out.
- **System:** the cell is the identity's atom: one cell per graduate on the impact page, rows of cells as dividers, week 1–12 progress. Risk: keep the gutters and avoid shading so it never tips into retro-game pixel art.

### 4. Live Cursor: codeweekend▌ (typographic-led)
- **Wordmark:** `codeweekend`, lowercase, Atkinson Hyperlegible Mono Bold (700), tracking 0 so every glyph sits on its monospaced cell. The identity is the type. It is followed directly by a Saffron-400 block cursor exactly one cell wide, from baseline to cap height, in the position of the next character. Someone is typing right now: fully live, instructor-led, and the code goes on.
- **Custom touch:** the counter of the "o" in "code" is redrawn as a vertical rhombus (a kilim lozenge), a quiet Afghan craft signature that shows from 24px. The e terminals stay open.
- **Colour:** Ink letters on Paper, Paper letters on Night, and the cursor always Saffron. Live-session pages only may add a 0.35-cell Pomegranate-600 dot superscript after the cursor ("live now"), never in the master logo.
- **Nav lockup:** about 20px font size in the 64px bar. **Monogram and avatar:** `cw▌` in Paper and Saffron on a Night square with 22% corner radius.
- **Favicon 16px:** a Night rounded square, a Paper "c" (2px stroke, pixel-hinted) and a Saffron 3×9px bar, reading "c▌". **Mono:** the cursor takes the letters' colour.
- **Motion:** the cursor blinks 3 times at 530ms on first load (header only), then holds. It is off under `prefers-reduced-motion`. The cost is one font and one rect.

### 5. Seed Circle: many make one
- **Symbol:** a lowercase "c" made of people as dots. Eight Lapis-600 dots sit on a circle of radius 10u at clockwise angles from 12 o'clock of 75°, 112.5°, 150°, 187.5°, 225°, 262.5°, 300° and 337.5°, leaving an opening near 1 o'clock. Dot diameters grow evenly from 3.0u (at 75°) to 3.8u (at 337.5°), so the ring gathers speed toward the gap. Just beyond the opening, a ninth, larger Saffron-400 dot (5u) sits at radius 12.5u, angle 30°, as if launched from the circle into the world. The circle is the community (learners, mentors in many countries, alumni, donors), and the Saffron dot is the graduate going further: propelled by many, never depicted. The seeds of one pomegranate are a secondary reading for copy.
- **Wordmark:** `codeweekend.`, lowercase Bricolage Grotesque 800, tracking -0.03em, in Ink (Paper on dark). The full stop is Saffron-400, echoing the launched dot.
- **Lockup:** the symbol is 36px in the nav, with the wordmark cap height at 50% of the symbol and a gap of one dot diameter.
- **Favicon 16px:** the eight dots merge into a continuous 3px 270° arc (Lapis-600 on Paper, Lapis-200 on Night) with a 4px Saffron dot at the opening.
- **Mono:** one colour, with the launched dot kept larger. **Flex:** campaigns may change the dot count (for example, a 30-dot ring for Demo Day 2026). The master stays at 8 + 1.
- **Risk:** dot rings are common in the non-profit sector, so ownability depends on the exact tangent launch and the size progression.
