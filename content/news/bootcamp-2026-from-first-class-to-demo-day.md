## Access that made the bootcamp possible {#access data-kicker="01 — Access"}

For many students in Afghanistan, the hardest part of an online bootcamp is not the code. It is showing up — on a working laptop, with a connection that holds through a live session, when power cuts, cost, and distance keep trying to pull you offline.

</div>

<div class="news-partners news-wide">
  <div class="news-partner news-partner--lead">
    <span class="news-partner__tag">Core funding partner</span>
    <strong class="news-partner__name">Linda Norgrove Foundation</strong>
    <p>Made the cohort itself possible. Their support covered internet access for learners and the wider costs of running the bootcamp — instruction, structure, and the operational runway that lets Afghan women and girls begin a cohort, complete it, and open the door for the next one.</p>
    <p class="news-partner__foot">A partnership that lasts before day one and after Demo Day.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="news-partner">
    <span class="news-partner__tag">Device &amp; connectivity partner</span>
    <strong class="news-partner__name">GiveInternet</strong>
    <ul class="news-partner__stats">
      <li><strong>10</strong><span>students received laptops</span></li>
      <li><strong>30+</strong><span>students received three months of internet support</span></li>
    </ul>
    <p>For those learners, the difference between watching from the doorway and sitting in the room.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="news-cols news-wide">
  <p class="news-pull">A device and a stable line decided who could stay.</p>
  <div>
    <p>The bootcamp was project-based and fully remote, so talent was never enough on its own. Without access, a strong student disappears from Zoom mid-lesson. With it, she finishes the week, asks the next question, and builds something with her name on it.</p>
    <p>Access is the first gate. What these students did after they had it is the rest of this story.</p>
  </div>
</div>

## The people behind the bootcamp {#team data-kicker="06 — The team"}

<div>

Instructors worked beyond the lecture. Mentors helped students size an idea, debug a failing feature, and decide what to do next. Guest speakers joined as practitioners — often the first time a student could picture herself in the field.

When someone missed sessions, the team looked for a way back in. Often the internet was down, or the power, or life at home made joining impossible that day. That quiet follow-up is a large part of why people finish.

</div>
