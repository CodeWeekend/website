## A program for women and girls in Afghanistan

The 2026 Web & AI Development Bootcamp was a 12-week, fully live program created for women and girls in Afghanistan. It combined computer and internet foundations, Git, HTML, CSS, JavaScript, React, applied AI, and a capstone presented at Demo Day.

| Measure               | Published result |
| --------------------- | ---------------: |
| Applications received |    More than 280 |
| Learners admitted     |               50 |
| Full scholarships     |               30 |
| Internet packages     |               24 |

These measures describe access and participation at the time of publication; they are not presented as graduation or employment totals.

## Removing barriers to participation

The [Linda Norgrove Foundation](https://lindanorgrovefoundation.org) funded 30 full scholarships and 24 internet packages. CodeWeekend also worked to provide laptops to learners who did not have a personal computer. That support addressed three practical barriers at once: tuition, connectivity, and access to equipment.

## Early student work

In the first weeks, **Mahdia Khamoosh**, from Panjshir, built and launched a personal website as a platform for her own voice. Other students, including **Elhama** and **Hananeh**, published movie-platform projects that displayed films and highlighted creative talent.

[Explore student projects](/projects/) and [read Mahdia's story](/stories/mahdia/).

## Becoming a nonprofit

In 2026, CodeWeekend incorporated as a nonprofit society in British Columbia, Canada. The structure supports accountable governance, long-term partnerships, and sustainable fundraising for future cohorts.

The original announcement remains available in the [news archive](/blog/becoming-nonprofit/).
